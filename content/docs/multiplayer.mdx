This page says what is known about mods in ICARUS, what is not known, and where Wax stands. The sources were
read on 7 October 2026 and are listed at the end.

## What is known

**ICARUS has no mode where players fight each other.** RocketWerkz describes it on its Steam page as "a solo
or multiplayer PvE survival game", for up to eight players who work together. In August 2021 RocketWerkz
wrote: "While we don't have a dedicated PVP feature, friendly fire does exist."

**No anti-cheat program was found.** The Steam page of ICARUS names none, and the game's folder, as Steam
installs it, holds no anti-cheat files. This says nothing about checks inside the game itself or on a
server.

**The game does have a notice about cheating in its own code.** Two of its functions are named
`NotifyOfCheater` and `CheatingNotification`. What sets them off is not known, so the game may tell the other
players in a session about some things a mod does.

**RocketWerkz has written about mods, and kindly.** These are from its own announcements on Steam:

- July 2022, under the heading "Modding Support Efforts": RocketWerkz had asked modders on Reddit what they
  need. One answer was to ship the game's debug files with every build. RocketWerkz wrote that such files
  are "useful to those making mods or cheats" and that "they will help a great deal with modding". The game
  comes with that file today.
- October 2022: a "special mention to the Icarus modding community", with a compliment on its work.
- December 2022: dedicated servers and save files kept on the player's PC let the community "make mods and
  conversions for the game".
- September 2023: the game got a check that "shows a message on the title screen if you have mods installed
  or your pak files from the patch are in a bad state". RocketWerkz described it as a help for finding the
  cause of crashes, and gave two launch options that switch the message off.

So RocketWerkz knows that players mod the game and has made some things easier for them. That is not the
same as permission for everything a mod can do.

## What is not confirmed

- **No licence terms of RocketWerkz's own were found for ICARUS.** The Steam page links no licence agreement
  for the game, and RocketWerkz's website links a privacy policy and no terms of use. There may be terms
  that were not found.
- **Nothing was found from RocketWerkz about script loaders such as UE4SS, or about Lua mods.** Its posts
  speak of mods in general, and of mods that come as pak files.
- **Nothing was found about mods that show what the game hides**, such as creatures or players behind walls.
- **Nothing was found about what happens to a player who uses mods in a shared game.** No rule about it was
  found.
- **RocketWerkz's Discord was not read for this page.** Something may have been said there.

One text does apply to every game on Steam, the Steam Subscriber Agreement. It calls a "Cheat" anything that
"may give a player an unfair competitive advantage when playing multiplayer versions" of a game, and says:
"You agree that you will not create Cheats or assist third parties in any way to create or use Cheats."
Whether a mod in a game where players work together falls under that is for Valve and RocketWerkz to say.
This page cannot settle it.

## Where Wax stands

- **Wax changes the game on the PC it is installed on.** Joining a game does not give Wax to the host or to
  the other players, and it does not ask them.
- **A mod can change the game, not only show things.** In a game you host, a mod can do what your own game
  allows. Giving an item and spawning a creature were tried that way and worked. In a game someone else
  hosts, their game decides, and most of that does nothing there.
- **Some mods show what the game hides.** Entity ESP outlines creatures and the other players in your
  session through walls, and puts a tag with a name over each. In a game you share, the others may not want
  you to have that, even when nobody plays against anybody.
- **Ask the people you play with.** On someone else's server the host decides. If they would rather you did
  not use a mod, switch it off with its **Enabled** switch on the Mods page of the Wax menu.
- **Alone, it is your game.** In a prospect of your own a mod affects nobody else.
- **The catalogue does not list mods made to harm other players.** [Publish a mod](/docs/publish-a-mod#review)
  has the rule.
- **The cheat and console mods of UE4SS are switched off in the download.** UE4SS comes with three small
  mods that open the game's developer console and its cheat commands: `CheatManagerEnablerMod`,
  `ConsoleCommandsMod` and `ConsoleEnablerMod`. Wax does not need them. To switch one on, change the `0`
  after its name to `1` in `ue4ss\Mods\mods.txt`. What this page says about playing with others goes for
  them too.

If RocketWerkz publishes rules about mods, those rules come first, and this page will be changed to say so.

## Host and guest

<NextVersion />

Whoever hosts a game runs its rules. Their game decides what is in the world, what a character carries and
how much health it has. `game.IsHost` is `true` when that is you: you play alone, or you host.

Wax has functions that change the world. They work for the host and are refused for a guest:

- changing a character: `Heal`, `Kill`, `Teleport`, assigning `Health`, `Stamina`, `Food`, `Water` and
  `Oxygen`, and putting a modifier on or taking it off ([Your character](/docs/character#what-the-host-can-change),
  [Stats and modifiers](/docs/stats#put-one-on-or-take-it-off));
- giving and taking items ([Items and inventories](/docs/items#give-and-take));
- spawning and changing creatures ([Creatures](/docs/creatures#spawn-a-creature));
- spawning things from a blueprint ([Blueprints](/docs/blueprints#spawn));
- setting the time of day ([Time, weather and the prospect](/docs/time-and-weather#set-the-time)).

As a guest, each of these raises an error at the line that called it, and the game is asked nothing. The error
says that only the host can do it. So a mod that offers such a thing should look at `game.IsHost` first:

```lua title="init.lua"
local window = ui.Window({ title = "Helper", width = 280, height = 130 })

window:Button("Heal me", function()
    if not game.IsHost then
        ui.Notify("Only the host can do this.", { kind = "warn" })
        return
    end
    game.Me:Heal()
end)
```

Changes to the [game's tables](/docs/tables#in-a-game-someone-else-hosts), which is what
[Recipes](/docs/recipes) and [The store](/docs/store) make, are different. As a guest they are made on your PC
only, and the host's game goes on working from its own tables.

Reading works for everybody, with what your PC knows. A guest's PC only knows the creatures near it, and it
does not have the list of all the weather on the map.

All of this was built and tried on the host of a session. None of it has been tried from a second PC that
joined a game: not the refusals, and not whether the other players see what a host's mod spawned or changed.
