Wax comes with one window of its own, called **Wax**. Press **F8** and it is there. It has six pages.

**F8** shows and hides this panel and nothing else. A mod's windows have a key of their own, which you
find on the mod's card of the Mods page. **Escape** hides everything that is up.

<NextVersion>In Wax 0.2.1, **F8** shows the panel together with every mod's windows. A card has no **Open
key** and no **Auto Update** switch of its own, and it has up and down arrows in place of
the six dots. The key setting is called **Menu key**.</NextVersion>

The line along the bottom is always visible. It says how many mods loaded and whether there are errors. On
the right it shows the frame rate and the time Wax uses per frame.

## Mods

<Shot
  src="panel-mods.png"
  alt="The Mods page. A search box and a refresh button sit above a card for the mod Hello 0.1.0, which shows Status loaded, Load time 5.0 ms, an Enabled switch that is on, and a Reload button."
/>

Every mod in the mods folder has a card here.

| On the card | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Status** | `loaded`, `switched off` or `failed`. A failed mod shows its error in red underneath. |
| **Load time** | How long the mod took to start. |
| **Enabled** | Switches the mod off and on. Wax remembers the choice. |
| **Auto Update** | Only on a mod that came from the catalogue. On, a newer version of that mod is put in by itself. Off, the card shows an **Update to** button when one is out. |
| **Open key** | Only on a mod that has a window. The key that shows and hides that mod's windows. |
| **Reload** | Starts the mod again from the top. |
| The six dots | At the left of the card's name. Hold them and drag the card to another place in the list. Mods load in this order. |
| Bin | Removes the mod, after asking. Its folder is renamed, not erased. [Get mods](/docs/get-mods) says how to bring it back. |

A card starts closed, unless its mod is new, has a problem or has an update to put in. Click its name to open
or close it. A name too long for the card is cut short with three dots. Rest the mouse on it to read all of
it.

### Put the mods in another order

<NextVersion>In Wax 0.2.1 the up and down arrows of a card move it.</NextVersion>

Hold the six dots of a card and move the mouse up or down. The card follows the mouse and the other cards
make room. Let go and the card stays there, and Wax remembers the order.

- The card stays inside the list while you hold it.
- Held near the top or the bottom of a long list, the list scrolls.
- An open card folds to its name while you hold it, and opens again where you put it down.
- **Escape** puts the card back where it was. So does letting go outside the list.
- A mod that needs another mod still loads after it. If you put it before that mod, the card goes back and a
  notification says why.
- With a search typed, you move the cards that show.

The search box filters the list. Type part of a name, or one of these words: `loaded`, `off`, `failed`, `new`.

A mod Wax has not seen before is listed as **new** and switched off. Flip **Enabled** to load it. This is so
whether the mod was added while the game ran or while it was closed.

**Look for updates** and **Check now**, above the cards, are for mods that came from the catalogue, and so is
the **Auto Update** switch on such a mod's card. [Get mods](/docs/get-mods#updates) says what they do.

### A key for each mod

<NextVersion />

Every mod that has a window has an **Open key** on its card. Press that key in the game to show the mod's
windows, and press it again to hide them. The Wax panel and the windows of other mods stay as they are, so
several can be up at once. The mouse is free while anything is up.

- A mod can ask for a key. For a mod that asks for none, Wax picks the first of F6, F9, F10, F11, F5, F4, F3
  and F2 that no window and no hotkey uses, and a notification tells you once which it is.
- If a mod later makes a hotkey on a key that Wax picked, Wax picks another one and tells you.
- To change it, click the key on the card and press the new one. A key can be held with Ctrl, Shift or Alt.
  Wax remembers your choice.
- A key that the Wax panel, another mod's windows or another mod's hotkey already uses is refused. A
  notification says who has it, and the key stays what it was.

## Log

<Shot
  src="panel-log.png"
  alt="The Log page. Three switches for Errors, Warnings and Prints, then a search box, a Follow switch, a copy button and a clear button. Below them the log shows lines such as wax.gui theme Midnight. At the bottom is the command bar with a history button and a run button."
/>

The log shows what Wax and your mods printed. The newest line is at the bottom.

- The **Errors**, **Warnings** and **Prints** switches choose which lines show.
- The search box keeps only lines that contain your text.
- **Follow** keeps the newest line in view. Switch it off to read older lines.
- Drag across lines to select them. **Ctrl+C** copies. **Ctrl+A** selects everything.
- The **copy** button copies every line that is showing.
- The **clear** button empties the view. The lines are hidden, not deleted.

Each line starts with who wrote it, in square brackets. Lines from your mod start with your mod's id.

### The command bar

The box at the bottom of the Log page runs Lua in the game. Type a line and press **Enter**.

```lua
print("Hello World")
```

If you type something that has a value, the log shows the value after an `=` sign.

```lua
game.MapName
```

```lua
1 + 1
```

More to try:

```lua
ui.Notify("Hi from the command bar")
```

This one needs a character, so run it in a prospect. It lists the parts your character is made of.

```lua
for i, child in ipairs(game.Character:GetChildren()) do print(i, child) end
```

What else the command bar does:

- **Tab** completes a name. Type `game.Ch` and a grey `aracter` appears. Press **Tab** to take it. Press **Tab** again for the next name that fits.
- After a dot, **Tab** lists what is there. Type `game.` and press **Tab**. The log lists the names you can use.
- The history button brings back the last command. Click again for the one before.
- The run button does the same as **Enter**.
- A command can wait. `task.wait(2) print("later")` works.

The command bar has every name a mod has (`game`, `ui`, `task` and so on), plus three extras:

| Name | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `mods.Hello` | The globals of the mod with the id `Hello`. |
| `exports.Hello` | What that mod's `init.lua` returned. |
| `clear()` | Removes everything your commands made, such as windows and signal connections. |

`require` does not work in the command bar. Reach a loaded mod through `mods` or `exports` instead.

### Running Lua from outside the game

The command bar is inside the game, so it always works. A line you type there runs with the same rights as a
mod.

A tool outside the game, such as the play button of the [VS Code extension](/docs/editor), can only run Lua
in the game in developer mode. A normal install does not take Lua from outside.
[The bridge and developer mode](/docs/security#the-bridge-and-developer-mode) says how to switch it on and
what that allows.

## Performance

This page shows how much of each frame Wax uses.

| On the page | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Frames per second**, **Frame time** | The game's own numbers. |
| **Wax per frame** | The time Wax and all mods use in each frame. |
| **Where it goes** | That time, split between the parts of Wax. The waiting and repeating code of your mods is counted under **Tasks**. |

The numbers are measured again every half second.

## Explorer

This page shows what is in the game while it runs, and lets you change it.

It has two sides. The left side lists what is in the game, and the right side shows what you picked. Both
are laid out alike, from the top down: a heading with its buttons, one small line, a search box, two
dropdowns side by side, one more row, and then the list.

### The left side

| From the top | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Explorer** and its button | The button closes every row that is open. |
| The small line | Says how much is listed. |
| The search box | Finds something by its name or its class. With several words, all of them must match. The cross in the box empties it. |
| The first dropdown | Picks what is listed: actors, creatures, players, items, buildings, components or everything. |
| The second dropdown | Sorts the list: by name, by class, nearest first or newest first. |
| The wide dropdown | Keeps only what is within a distance of you, from 25 to 500 metres. **Any distance from you** shows everything. |
| The list | The first rows are the parts of `game`: `Character`, `LocalPlayer`, `GameState`, `GameMode`, `GameInstance`, `Viewport` and `Engine`. Under **World** are the actors of the current world. |

A row with a small arrow has things inside it. Click the arrow to open the row. Click the row itself to pick
it.

### The right side

Until you pick something, the right side explains the page in four short lines.

| From the top | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| The name of what you picked | The button with the curved arrow goes back to what you had picked before. The copy button copies the Lua that reaches the object. |
| The small line | Shows that Lua. |
| The search box | Finds a member by its name or its type. |
| The first dropdown | Picks what is listed: **Properties**, **Functions**, **All** or **Changed**. |
| The second dropdown | Sorts the list: **By name** or **Changed first**. |
| The value row | Belongs to the member you pick in the table. It is described below. |
| The table | One row for each member: its name, its value and its type. The first row is the object's class, and its arrow shows what the class inherits from. |

The values are read again while you look, and a value that changed is marked for a moment. That is how you
find the property that goes with something you do in the game. Choose **Changed** to see only those, or
**Changed first** to put them at the top.

In the table:

- A value in colour is another object. Click it to go to that object.
- A true-or-false member has a switch at the end of its row.
- A vector, a rotator or a colour opens when you click it, and each part is a row of its own that you change
  like a number. Its arrow closes it again.
- A list opens with its arrow and shows what is in it. The places of a list are shown, not changed.
- A value that could not be read says so, and Wax leaves it alone.

### Change a value

Click a member in the table. The value row, above the table, now shows its name.

- For a value you can type, such as a number or a text, the row has a box. Type the new value and press
  **Enter**, or press the button with the tick.
- For anything else the row says in a few words why there is no box.
- The `<>` button copies the Lua that reads the member and the Lua that sets it.

Choose **Functions** to see the object's functions with their parameters. They are not called from this
page. The `<>` button copies the call.

**Changes as Lua** appears under both sides after your first change. It lists everything you changed here as
lines of Lua that do the same. **Copy** takes them for a mod, and **Clear** empties the list.

In a narrow window one side shows at a time, and a **List** button at the top of the right side takes you
back.

<Callout type="warn" title="A change here is a change in your game">
  Not every value is meant to be changed. Try things on a character you can afford to lose.
</Callout>

## Settings

<Shot
  src="panel-settings.png"
  alt="The Settings page with five rows: Menu key F8, Theme Midnight, Accent colour From the theme, an Animations switch that is on, and Interface size 100%."
/>

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Wax panel key** | The key that shows and hides the Wax panel. Click it, then press a new key. It is **F8** until you change it. A mod's windows have their own key, on the mod's card of the [Mods](#mods) page. |
| **Theme** | The colours of every window. See [Themes](/docs/gui/themes). |
| **Accent colour** | The highlight colour. `From the theme` uses the theme's own. |
| **Animations** | Switches the small movements on and off. |
| **Interface size** | Makes everything larger or smaller. The list holds the sizes that text can still be read at on your screen, so a small game window offers fewer of them. |

These settings are remembered the next time you play.

## Add your own page

A mod can add a page to the Wax panel. Use it for tools you need while testing.

```lua title="init.lua"
local page = ui.Debug.Page("My mod", { icon = "wrench" })

page:Title("My mod", "Tools for testing.")

page:Button("Say hi", function()
    ui.Notify("Hi from my page.")
end)

page:Button("Print the map", function()
    print(game.MapName)
end)
```

The page goes away when your mod reloads, and comes back when `init.lua` runs again.

Write `ui.Debug.Page` with a dot, not a colon.

| Function | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `ui.Debug.Page(name, options)` | Adds a page and returns it. Add controls to it as you would to a window. |
| `ui.Debug.Show()` | Shows the Wax panel and frees the mouse. Your own windows stay as they are. |
| `ui.Debug.Window()` | The panel's window, or `nil` while the panel is not running. |
