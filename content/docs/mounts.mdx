Three things for mods that deal with animals you ride: a mount made in one call, a creature that wears another
model, and a mount that attacks when you tell it to. Together they are what a mod needs to add a mount of its own
to the game.

```lua title="init.lua"
task.spawn(function()
    local mount, why = game.Creatures:SpawnMount("Mount_Moa", { name = "Dusty" })
    if not mount then
        print("No mount:", why)
        return
    end
    print("Your mount is here. Walk up to it and ride.")
end)
```

## A mount in one call

`game.Creatures:SpawnMount(kind, place, options)` puts a tamed animal into the world.
`kind` is the animal's set-up row, such as `"Mount_Moa"` or `"Mount_Horse"`, or a kind such as `"Moa"`.

It does what the game does when a taming ends, and then the two things a player would do by hand:

1. The animal is made with the game's own spawn, a few metres in front of you or at the place you give.
2. It gets its name and you become its owner.
3. A saddle goes into its saddle slot. That is what makes the game give a mount its seat. Without a saddle the
   game does not offer to ride it.

After that it is an ordinary mount of the game: the game's own prompt to ride it works, it follows you, and it is
saved with the prospect.

| Option | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `name` | The mount's name. |
| `saddle` | The saddle item it wears. `"Saddle_Standard"` when left out, `false` for none. |
| `owner` | `false` leaves it with no owner, to be claimed in the game. |
| `facing` | Which way it looks, as `{ Yaw = 90 }`. Toward you when left out. |

Inside a task the call answers once the saddle is on. When the saddle did not go on, the mount still comes back,
with the reason as a second value. Outside a task it answers at once and finishes a moment later.

Where the game knows no ground to walk on, as on a platform a mod built high in the air, the mount is made on the
ground below and brought up to the place.

## Another body

`creature:SetModel(model)` gives a creature another skeletal mesh to wear, with its materials. The creature goes
on walking, running and being ridden as before.

```lua
local content = mod.Content

local function dress(creature)
    creature:SetModel({
        mesh = content:Load("SK_Strider"),
        materials = { [1] = content:Load("M_Strider") },
        fur = false,
        saddle = false,
    })
end

for _, creature in ipairs(game.Creatures:GetTamed("Moa")) do
    if not creature.HasModel then dress(creature) end
end
```

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `mesh` | The skeletal mesh: from `game.Assets:Load`, from `mod.Content:Load`, or a path. |
| `materials` | Materials by slot, 1 first. A slot you leave out shows what the mesh itself names. |
| `fur` | `false` hides the creature's coat of fur or feathers. The game grows it on a body of its own and would draw it over the new one. |
| `saddle` | `false` hides the saddle the game shows on a mount, for a model that has one of its own. The rider still sits on it. |

Three rules keep this safe:

- **The mesh has to be on the creature's own skeleton.** A creature's animations belong to one skeleton. A mesh
  built on another one would stand still, so `SetModel` refuses it and names both skeletons.
- **Wax puts the model back.** The game writes a mount's own skin again now and then, a second after it appears
  and whenever its looks are worked out again. Wax looks once a second and puts your model back on.
- **It undoes itself.** When your mod unloads, or on `creature:ResetModel()`, the creature has its own body
  again. `creature.HasModel` says whether it wears one.

`SetModel` works for a client as well as for the host. Each player sees it only in their own game, so everybody
who should see the model needs the mod.

## A mount that strikes

`mount:Strike()` makes a mount attack. It is what the game does when the rider of a mount attacks:
the mount plays its own attack, pays the stamina for it, and hits what stands in front of it at the strike. The
game keeps that for mounts with the talent for it. `Strike` asks for no talent.

```lua
local mine = nil    -- the mount you ride, kept by your mod

ui.Hotkey("LeftMouseButton", function()
    if ui.IsOpen() or not mine or not mine:IsValid() then return end
    local started, why = mine:Strike()
    if not started then print("Not now:", why) end
end)
```

It answers `false` and the reason while the mount is in the middle of another move, and for a kind of mount the
game gives no attack.

## A model of your own

The mesh for `SetModel` comes from a pak made with the Unreal editor ([Game content of your own](/docs/game-content)).
A model from a 3D program has no bones the game knows, so it has to be bound to the skeleton of the animal it
replaces. These are the steps the Strider, the example mount, was made with:

1. **Pick the animal.** Take one whose shape is close: two legs for two legs, four for four. Its skeleton decides
   which bones your model can move, and its set-up row is what you pass to `SpawnMount`.
2. **Place the joints.** For each bone of that skeleton, mark where the same joint is in your model: hips, knees,
   ankles, the line of the spine, the neck, the head. Lay them through the model as the animal stands in the
   game, not as its skeleton lies at rest.
3. **Reshape to the bone lengths.** The game's animations carry the animal's own bone lengths. A leg that is
   longer or shorter than the animal's gets pushed out at its joints. So stretch each part of the model evenly
   between its two joints until the joints sit where the animal's are, and bind that shape.
4. **Bind and export.** Give every point of the model its bones, with a soft blend at each joint, and write an FBX
   whose bones have the names and the turns of the game's skeleton.
5. **Import onto the game's skeleton.** In the Unreal project, import the FBX as a skeletal mesh and choose the
   game's own skeleton asset for it. A copy of the skeleton in your mod's folder does not work: the creature's
   animations only run on the one the game has.
6. **Use the game's creature material.** A material built in the stock editor draws grey in ICARUS. Make yours a
   material instance of the game's creature material and set its three pictures: `Color`, `Normal` and `MRO`.
7. **Cook and load.** Build the pak, then `mod.Content:Load` and `SetModel`.

To look at the result before it walks around, show it in a window with the animal's own animations:

```lua
local content = mod.Content
content:Load("SK_Strider")

local window = ui.Window({ title = "Strider", width = 344, height = 420 })
local view = window:Model({ size = 300, backdrop = true })
view:Show({
    mesh = content:Path("SK_Strider"),
    idle = "/Game/ASS/CRE/Moa/Anims/Moa_Idle_01",
    walk = "/Game/ASS/CRE/Moa/Anims/Moa_Walk",
    animation = "walk",
})
```

Load the mesh with `mod.Content:Load` before you show it by its path.

## What it does not do

- Parts of the model with no bone in the animal's skeleton stay stiff. A Moa has no wing bones, so wings on a
  model bound to it do not flap.
- The mount keeps the animal's own stats, sounds and talents. To change numbers, change the animal's rows
  ([Tables](/docs/tables)).
