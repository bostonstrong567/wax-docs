Blueprints

Describe a thing in Lua, with parts, meshes, materials and functions, and spawn it into the world.

A blueprint describes a kind of thing: which parts it has, what they show, and which of your functions run for it. blueprint:Spawn then puts one into the world. No file is made: a thing is an actor of a class the game already has, put together from your description.

Parts

A thing is made of parts. A part is a component of the game with what it shows and where it sits.

In a partWhat it is
meshWhat it shows: the path of a mesh of the game, or a shape from game.Assets:Mesh.
classThe component class, such as "PointLightComponent". Left out, it follows from the mesh.
materialThe material of its first slot: a path, a material, or a table that says what to make.
atWhere it sits, measured from the thing: { 0, 0, 100 } is one metre up.
rotationHow it is turned, in degrees: { Yaw = 45 }.
scaleIts size against the size its mesh has by itself. One number, or one for each direction.
collision"none": things pass through. "solid": things stop at it, which is what a mesh part does when nothing is said. "touch": things pass through and your touched function hears of them.
setProperties of the component, by name, written before the game starts using it.
roottrue for the part the others hang on. It stands where the thing is spawned.
  • Give the parts by name, as above, or as a list in which each has a name. A blueprint can have 64.
  • With parts given by name, say which one is the root when more than one has a place.
  • Paths and option names are checked when you call Define. A wrong path or a misspelt option raises an error there, with the nearest right name, and not at the first spawn.

Spawn

blueprint:Spawn(position, rotation, data) puts one in the world and gives you the thing.

  • position is { X = , Y = , Z = } or { x, y, z }. rotation is a turn in degrees and may be left out.
  • data is a table of your own that the thing keeps as thing.Data.
  • A mistake while the thing is put together raises an error and leaves nothing standing.
On a thingWhat it is
thing.DataYour own table for this thing.
thing.PositionWhere it is. Assign a position to move it there at once.
thing.FacingHow it is turned, in degrees. Assign a turn to set it.
thing.Alivetrue until the thing is gone. Reading it never raises an error.
thing.ActorThe Instance of its actor, for functions that take an Instance.
thing.BlueprintThe blueprint it came from.
thing:Part(name)The Instance of one of its parts.
thing:AddPart(name, part)Makes one more part on it.
thing:GetBounds()The box all its parts fit in.
thing:Destroy()Takes it out of the world with all its parts.

A thing also has everything its actor has: thing.Name, thing:IsA("Actor"), and the actor's own properties and functions.

blueprint:GetThings() lists the things of a blueprint that are in the world, and blueprint:Count() counts them. game.Blueprints:GetThings() lists the things of every blueprint.

Functions that run for a thing

In the blueprintWhen it runs
began = function(thing)Once, when the thing is in the world with its parts.
stepped = function(thing, dt)Every frame, with the seconds since it last ran for that thing.
every = { 0.5, function(thing, dt) end }At its own pace: the seconds between two runs, then the function.
touched = function(thing, other)When an actor starts to overlap a part with collision = "touch".
ended = function(thing, reason)Once, a frame after the thing has gone. Only Data, Blueprint and Alive can be read then.
  • All stepped and every functions together get 2 ms of a frame. What is left over waits a frame.
  • The time is real time, so stepped also runs while the game is paused.
  • ended does not run when the blueprint is removed or your mod unloads.

What does not last

  • The game does not save things. They are gone after a map change, after leaving the prospect and after a restart. Spawn them again, for example on game.MapChanged. The blueprint itself stays through a map change.
  • When your mod unloads or reloads, its things are destroyed and its blueprints forgotten.
  • Defining a name again replaces the blueprint, and the things of the old one are destroyed.

What has been seen

The crate above was spawned on the host of a prospect: three crates, each turning from Lua, with their lamps lit. Defining took 1 to 2 ms, a spawn about half a millisecond. These have not been looked at yet:

  • Whether the other players of a session see a thing.
  • A base class other than "Actor". base takes another class by name, or a blueprint class of the game by its path, and then the thing is that actor. Only "Actor" has been seen working.
  • touched and collision = "touch", and moving a thing by assigning thing.Position.
  • Part classes other than a mesh part, a shape part and a point light.

While no blueprint is defined, this costs nothing. With things in the world and no functions given, a frame costs one question to the game at most.

Every function is listed in the Blueprints reference.

Assets

game.Assets loads the game's meshes, textures and materials by path, makes materials of your own, and builds shapes from numbers.

Animations, effects and sounds

Play the game's own clips, write animations as keys in Lua for anything, and add particles, lights, trails, shakes and sounds.
